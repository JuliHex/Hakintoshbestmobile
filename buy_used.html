Buying a used laptop

The best Hackintosh laptops are 2018–2020 business machines, which are now cheap second-hand. Here's where to look and how to avoid a dud.

Where to look

  • Refurbishers and off-lease resellers. Ex-corporate ThinkPads, Latitudes and EliteBooks, usually tested, cleaned and with a short warranty. Often the best value.
  • eBay and similar marketplaces. Widest choice. Buy from sellers with a returns policy and clear photos of the actual unit.
  • Local classifieds. Cheapest, and you can test before paying. Bring a USB stick (see the checklist).
  • Manufacturer outlets. Lenovo, Dell and HP sell refurbished stock with full warranties, though newer models may not be Hackintosh-friendly.

What to check in the listing

SSDPlan to swap known-problem NVMe drives (e.g. Samsung PM981) for a compatible one.
BIOS lockSupervisor or Computrace-locked business laptops can be impossible to configure. Ask the seller.
Battery healthAsk for the wear level. A replacement battery is cheap for some models and painful for others.

In-person test checklist

  1. Boot into the BIOS and confirm there's no supervisor password. Note the BIOS version.
  2. Boot a Linux live USB and run lspci and lsusb to identify the Wi-Fi card, audio codec and trackpad.
  3. Test every key, the trackpad buttons, all USB ports, the headphone jack and the webcam.
  4. Check the screen for dead pixels, backlight bleed and hinge wobble.
  5. Run on battery for 10 minutes and watch how fast the percentage drops.
  6. Check the charger's wattage matches the original spec.
